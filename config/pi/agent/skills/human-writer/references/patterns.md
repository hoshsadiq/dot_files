# Pattern Catalog

Use this reference as a checklist when a draft still feels machine-shaped after the first pass. The goal is not to follow every item mechanically. It is to catch the patterns that make prose feel generic, inflated, or over-engineered.

## 1. AI vocabulary

Words to watch: `delve`, `crucial`, `pivotal`, `vibrant`, `vital`, `foster`, `showcase`, `underscore`, `interplay`, `garner`, `enhance`, `renowned`, `nestled`, `additionally`.

Phrases to watch: `in the realm of`, `excited to announce`, `let's dive in`, `plays a key role`, `commitment to excellence`, `it's important to note`, `in conclusion`.

Prefer simpler substitutes:

| AI version | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| serves as | is |
| stands as | is |
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | help |
| leverage | use |
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| has the ability to | can |

## 2. Significance inflation

AI often turns a plain fact into a major turning point.

Before:
> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain.

After:
> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was established in 1989 to collect and publish regional statistics independently from Spain's national statistics office.

Watch for: `stands as`, `serves as`, `is a testament`, `is a reminder`, `crucial role`, `pivotal role`, `underscores`, `highlights`, `reflects broader`, `symbolizing`, `setting the stage for`, `represents a shift`, `evolving landscape`, `indelible mark`, `deeply rooted`.

## 3. Promotional language

AI drifts into brochure mode.

Before:
> Nestled within the breathtaking region of Gonder, Alamata stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural heritage and stunning natural beauty.

After:
> Alamata is a town in the Gonder region of Ethiopia, known for its weekly market and 18th-century church.

Watch for: `boasts`, `vibrant`, `rich` in a figurative sense, `profound`, `enhancing`, `showcasing`, `commitment to`, `natural beauty`, `nestled`, `in the heart of`, `groundbreaking`, `renowned`, `breathtaking`, `must-visit`, `stunning`.

## 4. Superficial -ing padding

Watch for chains of participles that pad out the sentence without adding real information.

Before:
> The temple's colors resonate with the region's beauty, symbolizing local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast, reflecting the community's deep connection to the land.

After:
> The temple uses blue, green, and gold. The architect said these reference local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast.

Watch for: `highlighting`, `underscoring`, `emphasizing`, `ensuring`, `reflecting`, `symbolizing`, `contributing to`, `cultivating`, `fostering`, `encompassing`, `showcasing`.

## 5. Vague attributions

Name the source when you can.

Before:
> Experts believe it plays a crucial role in the regional ecosystem.

After:
> The river supports several endemic fish species, according to a 2019 survey by the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

`Graphite found` is better than `studies show`. Named people, organizations, dates, and numbers make claims easier to trust.

## 6. Copula avoidance

AI sometimes dodges `is` and `are` with clunky alternatives.

Before:
> Gallery 825 serves as LAAA's exhibition space. The gallery features four spaces and boasts over 3,000 square feet.

After:
> Gallery 825 is LAAA's exhibition space. The gallery has four rooms totaling 3,000 square feet.

Watch for: `serves as`, `stands as`, `marks`, `represents`, `functions as`, `boasts`, `features`, `offers`.

## 7. Rule of three

Three-item lists are not wrong, but AI reaches for them too often.

Before:
> The event features keynote sessions, panel discussions, and networking opportunities. Attendees can expect innovation, inspiration, and industry insights.

After:
> The event includes talks and panels. There's also time for informal networking between sessions.

## 8. Negative parallelisms and contrastive negation

`Not only X but Y` and `It's not just X, it's Y` can sound inflated when the distinction is not real.

Before:
> It's not just about the beat; it's part of the aggression and atmosphere. It's not merely a song, it's a statement. The real question isn't whether we should adopt AI, it's whether we can afford not to.

After:
> The heavy beat adds to the aggressive tone. We plan to adopt AI tools next quarter.

Use these forms only when they make an actual distinction.

## 9. Elegant variation

AI often cycles synonyms when plain repetition would sound more natural.

Before:
> The protagonist faces challenges. The main character must overcome obstacles. The central figure eventually triumphs. The hero returns home.

After:
> The protagonist faces many challenges but eventually triumphs and returns home.

## 10. False ranges

Avoid ranges that are decorative rather than meaningful.

Before:
> Our journey has taken us from the singularity of the Big Bang to the grand cosmic web, from the birth of stars to the enigmatic dance of dark matter.

After:
> The book covers the Big Bang, star formation, and current theories about dark matter.

## 11. Em dash overuse

AI uses em dashes more than most people do.

Before:
> The term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions - not by the people themselves. You don't say "Netherlands, Europe" - yet this continues - even in official documents.

After:
> The term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions, not by the people themselves.

## 12. Inline-header lists and boldface

Lists that shout their own labels can read like machine-edited copy.

Before:
> - **User Experience:** The UX has been significantly improved.
> - **Performance:** Performance has been enhanced through optimized algorithms.
> - **Security:** Security has been strengthened with end-to-end encryption.

After:
> The update improves the interface, speeds up load times through optimized algorithms, and adds end-to-end encryption.

Prefer prose or simple lists when the labels are not doing real work.

## 13. Filler, hedging, and generic conclusions

Cut sentences that explain nothing.

Before:
> It is important to note that the data shows the system has the ability to process more requests.

After:
> The data shows the system can process more requests.

Before:
> It could potentially possibly be argued that the policy might have some effect.

After:
> The policy may affect outcomes.

Before:
> The future looks bright. Exciting times lie ahead as we continue this journey toward excellence.

After:
> The company plans to open two more locations next year.

## 14. Communication artifacts

Remove chatbot residue that slipped into the draft.

Watch for: `Great question!`, `I hope this helps!`, `Let me know if you'd like...`, `Certainly!`, `Of course!`, `You're absolutely right!`, `As of my last training update...`, `While specific details are limited based on available information...`.

## 15. Pseudo-profound flourishes

State what something does. Skip the grand framing.

Watch for: `This changes everything.`, `The implications are staggering.`, `We're witnessing the emergence of...`, `This is just the beginning.`, `The future of X is Y.`

## 16. Dying metaphors

Worn-out metaphors often read as filler.

Avoid: `toe the line`, `Achilles' heel`, `level playing field`, `play into the hands of`, `hotbed`, `at the end of the day`.

Prefer simple verbs when they do the job.

## 17. Staccato and monotone rhythm

Too many short declarative sentences in a row can feel robotic. Too much polished rhythm can feel equally staged.

Before:
> The experiment produced interesting results. The agents generated 3 million lines of code. Some developers were impressed. Others were skeptical. The implications remain unclear.

After:
> I genuinely don't know how to feel about this one. 3 million lines of code, generated while the humans presumably slept. Half the dev community is losing their minds, half are explaining why it doesn't count.

## 18. Extrapolation to universal principles

Keep the claim at the scale of the evidence.

Before:
> Once you experience fast deploys, slow CI feels completely unacceptable. After switching to TypeScript, going back to plain JavaScript feels wrong.

After:
> Fast deploys cut our release cycle from two weeks to a day. We switched to TypeScript because it caught three bugs in the first week.

State what happened. Skip the universal lesson unless the source really supports it.

## Quick reminders

- Read aloud before you stop.
- Favor specifics over abstractions when the facts are available.
- Keep helpful repetition if it sounds human.
- Add voice sparingly and only where the context benefits from it.
